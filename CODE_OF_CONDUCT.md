# Code of conduct

om is a small project run by one maintainer, and it is shared in the open. This page says how people
treat each other in its issues, pull requests, discussions and any other space that carries its name.
It is short on purpose: be decent, and assume the other person is too.

## What we expect

- Be respectful. Disagree with the idea, not the person.
- Be patient with newcomers and with questions that look basic. Everyone starts somewhere.
- Be specific and kind in review: say what to change and why.
- Assume good faith, and ask before assuming otherwise.
- Keep to the topic: om, its code, its docs and its use.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, personal attacks, trolling or demeaning comments, including about someone's
  identity, background, experience or language skills.
- Sexualised language or attention, and unwelcome contact.
- Publishing other people's private information (addresses, hosts, tokens, emails) without their consent.
- Spam, advertising unrelated to the project, and repeated off-topic demands for attention.
- Anything else a reasonable person would call hostile in a professional setting.

## Security reports

A vulnerability is not a conduct matter: follow `SECURITY.md`, and keep the details out of public issues.

## Who this covers

Everyone taking part: users, contributors, reviewers and the maintainer. It applies in project spaces,
and outside them when someone is clearly acting as part of the project or targeting a participant.

## Reporting and what happens next

To report something, open a private report at
<https://github.com/Chitakasa/om/security/advisories/new> and start the title with "Conduct". Only the
maintainer sees it. Say what happened and where, with links. Reports are read promptly and treated as
confidential, and the reporter is not named to the person reported.

The maintainer decides the response, in proportion to what happened: a reminder, a request to edit or
remove something, a temporary or permanent ban from the project's spaces, or no action when nothing
was wrong. The maintainer steps aside from any report about themselves; in that case the reporter may
instead contact GitHub Support. A decision can be appealed once, in the same private thread.

## Where this comes from

This is a short, plain-language page in the spirit of the Contributor Covenant (<https://www.contributor-covenant.org>),
which is worth reading if you want a longer, widely used model.
